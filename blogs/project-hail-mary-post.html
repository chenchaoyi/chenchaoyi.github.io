An Unremarkable School Teacher Who Saved Earth With An Alien

Posted on May 2026

—A movie review of Project Hail Mary

Watched in March 2026

Rating: 8.9/10

Less than two hours prior to writing this review, I watched Project Hail Mary at the movie theater and came away unaffected, reflective and just a bit bewildered. Because despite the seemingly surface-level edifice of this highly anticipated film, it is much more than that, exploring the much in-depth yearning of the human race and the transcendingly mortal instinct to survive, though the execution of both is arguably underwhelming.

<! Spoil Alert Starts>

Project Hail Mary, inspired by the novel from Andy Weir, centers a perfectly unremarkable high-school teacher Ryland Grace who, because of a controversial science paper he wrote years ago, was invited by government leader Eva Stratt to involve in an obscure international mission that aims to save Earth’s dying sun. After contributing his observations while studying a cell sample collected from the Astrophage—the organism responsible not only for feeding on the sun’s stellar energy, but various planets’; Ryland finds himself swept up in a desperate governmental mission called Hail Mary that aims to send three trained astronauts to the only uncontaminated planet for research, both as a suicide mission—due to the lack of fuel for a return journey, and as humanity’s last hope for survival.

The movie opens with Ryland waking up in said Hail Mary spaceship light-years away from Earth, experiencing temporary amnesia from an induced coma, and realizing that all of his crew members are dead. Along with the flashbacks lightly interwoven throughout the film, we watch as Ryland somehow gains communication with a spider-like alien “Rocky” whose fellow space crew died from mishandled radiation, and who aims to save his own planet. Eager to collaborate with each other both for company and for heightened efficiency, Ryland and Rocky develop a firm friendship that holds through climactic space action scenes, scientific breakthroughs and an unexpected yet picturesque ending. Ryland sets on a journey of self-discovery as he saves humankind and unveils the truth behind why he became one of the astronauts abord Hail Mary.

<! Spoil Alert Ends>

Along with climactic music, soaring visual effects and decent acting, this movie was held up by the flashbacks interwoven between dragging space scenes that provided reflective and emotional breathing room. A scene in which Eva Stratt sang “Sign of the times” as a karaoke cover at a party elusively and vulnerably highlighted the theme of the story, while deepening the emotional impact of some of her actions later on in the film. It was an excellent scene that provided a break away from the often tension-wrought shots of space action and alien communication—e.g. the many visually impressive yet boring shots of Ryland’s spaceship—that dragged on for way too long.

A dry sense of humor overarches the entire movie, the whiplash effect of which between Ryan Gosling and Rocky, as Gosling’s bouncing board, is carefully balanced and usually does not come off as needy or exhausting. The humor was definitely a more prominent element of the plot than I expected, but since it didn’t seem to have an obviously slowing effect on the pacing, I thought this was decent commercial touch.

Despite the dry sense of humor and crackling flashbacks, I found myself consistently exhausted during the first hour of the movie. As I did not read the original book, all of my context for the movie had to be provided by it, and, unfortunately, that was a major let-down. Very brief and elusive explanations for the “Astrophage” were not enough to supply for many dominant science-oriented scenes that came later, notably the science behind the communication tunnel built between Ryland and Rocky. It seems as if this movie is trying way too hard to pick up on the “show don’t tell” literary advice, except that doesn’t work in this context due to the movie’s target audience and filming style. This largely took away from my viewing experience and the pacing only picked up after Rocky came along, but even then the movie could not seem to decide on one single climactic point. Every half-hour or so, an emotional peak powered by very decent music comes along, which, in spite of the visual and audible decorum, distracted from the central storyline and theme.

The movie has two themes: friendship and bravery. The rapidly developing friendship between Ryan Gosling and Rocky takes center stage, halting the pacing and bewildering the central message. Meanwhile, the second theme is one that I can truly appreciate and that isn’t a cliché: the human desire of exploration. Not many sci-fi blockbusters convey this theme, but Project Hail Mary does so, and it does so beautifully. The feeling of ecstasy, wonder and dread that abounded when Ryland stepped onto alien soil, and when he stared back at a truth much larger than himself—the universe itself, was perfectly illustrated through creative cinematography and emotional acting. The two clashing themes were not cleverly interwoven and the overall message feels confused and chaotic, without a dominant spine to connect each individual sub-theme. In the case of Instellar, the spine had been time, and with Martian, it had been the instinctual desire to survive. In the case of Project Hail Mary, there had been two, which took away from the feeling of organic wholeness.

Regardless, this movie flaunted lovely character development and decent emotional punches that altogether form a very decent and sweeping commercial blockbuster. Although it is certainly not comparable to Instellar and, in my opinion, is a less organic viewing experience than Martian, I did appreciate the obvious and ostensible effort that went into the assembly of such powerful flashbacks and music, though the commercial execution of all of which will never position this movie as a memorable or remarkable experience in my mind.

Overall, Project Hail Mary is a fun, dramatic, entertaining blockbuster that does get slow at some points but that was saved by its reflective flashbacks and significant music. On the scale of one to ten, I would give it a decent 8.9, and if you are ever wrung and need to kick back one day with chips and an escapist sci-fi, then this is one that I will vehemently recommend.
